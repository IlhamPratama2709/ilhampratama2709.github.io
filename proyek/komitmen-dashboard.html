Summary

KOMITMEN is a research programme at Universitas Islam Internasional Indonesia with seven workstreams. I built this site so that its eight dashboards sit in one place and a reader can start from a district or province as well as from a topic.

Kota Semarang shows the result. Its page draws figures from all eight dashboards, and its PDF report runs to 36 A4 pages. Every other district and province has a page and report in the same layout, 552 in all.

A reader can go from a national topic to one village in four steps

The videos follow that route. They have no sound and play when scrolled into view. The site is in Indonesian, and each video carries English captions.

The dashboards grew out of research on three workstreams

Each dashboard began as working material for a study. Three of those studies shaped most of what the site shows.

Stream 2 · Stranded assets

Fast subsidence is rare, but 3.35 million people live on it

This stream asks when coal, palm oil, and land lose their economic value before the end of their planned life. Ground sinking faster than 5 cm a year appears in only 11 of the 119 districts in Java, and 3.35 million people live on it.

I combined radar estimates from Ohenhen et al. (2026) with village boundaries, population, night-time light, PODES flood reports, and land cover, so each village has one record. The next question is whether coastal land and buildings in Semarang lose value, and whether owners, banks, or local property-tax revenue bear the loss.

A second dashboard sets the regional development plans of 38 provinces and 514 districts beside field data: 521 coal mining sites, 280 coal power plants, and 1,227 palm oil mills.

Stream 3 · Growth diagnostics

A database of 514 districts shows what holds growth back in each place

This stream applies the Hausmann–Rodrik–Velasco framework at district level, asking which constraint binds growth in each place. I built the database behind it: 95 variables for 514 districts, drawn from PODES 2024, Susenas March 2025, regional finance records, and Bank Indonesia statistics. A two-year panel adds 1,028 observations for 2021 and 2024.

The power plant dashboard came from the same work. It maps 3,123 plants with 104.7 GW of installed capacity from BIG, Global Energy Monitor, and news reports, and it compares the totals with the Energy Ministry's official figures.

Stream 6 · Industrial decarbonisation

Six of 16 years of plant energy data could not be read as a trend

This stream studies green industrial policy for net zero by 2050. Its dashboard covers nine energy-intensive subsectors from the BPS survey of large and medium manufacturers, 2000–2015, along with 143 industrial estates and 25 special economic zones.

We checked the energy figures plant by plant before drawing any chart. Recorded electricity use at identical plants fell 39 percent in 2010 and rose 86 percent in 2014, while median employment did not move. That pattern points to reporting, not production. So the dashboard compares sectors within a year and uses intensity per plant, and it does not show energy use as a trend.

My role

I built the site, including the home page, region pages, and PDF reports, and I built the dashboards. The green jobs dashboard is the exception: a colleague built it.

I found and compiled the data for three of the eight dashboards. For the others, the data came from the team, and my part was building and refining the dashboard. The household welfare dashboard, for example, uses Susenas March 2025 data processed by a colleague.
