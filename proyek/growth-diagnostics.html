Almost every district in Indonesia has electricity, but in some provinces the outages last 65 times as long

This stream asks which constraint holds back growth in each of Indonesia's 514 districts. It starts with the power supply: where the plants are, what they burn, and how often the lights go out.

Summary

Growth diagnostics looks for the one constraint that binds hardest in a place, instead of listing everything a place lacks. This stream applies the method of Hausmann, Rodrik, and Velasco to each of Indonesia's 514 districts.

Power supply is the first branch examined. Access is close to universal: in the median district, 99.4 percent of families use PLN electricity. Reliability is not. In 2024 a customer in Lampung lost power for 27.8 hours on average, against 0.4 hours in Jakarta.

The stream has built the district database and the power plant map. Which constraint binds in which district is still being tested.

The framework asks whether growth is held back by low returns or by costly finance

The method starts from a simple question: is private investment low because it does not pay, or because finance is too expensive? Each branch then points to indicators a district can be compared on.

GeographyTerrain, coastal and island villages, and natural disasters
Human capitalSchooling, age structure, urbanisation, and unemployment
InfrastructureElectrification, outages, paved roads, mobile internet, water, and sanitation
Local financeBank loans and deposits relative to regional GDP
GovernmentFiscal capacity and the share of own revenue in the budget

One database brings 95 indicators for 514 districts together

The database draws on PODES 2024, the village census; Susenas, the household survey; Sakernas, the labour force survey; regional finance records; Bank Indonesia statistics; and PLN's 2024 statistics. A two-year panel adds 1,028 observations for 2021 and 2024.

Each indicator sits at the level its source reports. Outages and bank lending exist only by province, so every district in a province carries the province figure.

Electricity access is nearly universal; roads and sanitation are not

Only 88 of 514 districts have fewer than 90 percent of families on PLN electricity. Sanitation is the widest gap: in 345 districts, fewer than 90 percent of households have adequate sanitation.

A customer in Lampung lost power for 65 times as long as one in Jakarta

In 2024 the average PLN customer in Lampung was without power for 27.8 hours, in 6.3 separate outages. In Jakarta the figure was 0.4 hours. Bangka Belitung, with 0.9 hours, did nearly as well.

Outages, unlike access, separate districts that otherwise look alike. That makes them a better candidate for a binding constraint, especially for firms that cannot run without power.

The data are coarse. PLN reports 21 of the 34 provincial figures for units that cover more than one province, so neighbours such as North, Central, and Gorontalo in Sulawesi share one number.

Coal provides 59 percent of operating capacity, and captive plants a quarter

The power plant map holds 3,123 plants, of which 3,087 still operate, with 104.7 GW of capacity. Coal provides 62.2 GW of that and gas 25.8 GW. Renewables together add up to 11 percent.

A quarter of the capacity never reaches the public grid. Captive plants built by mines, smelters, and factories for their own use hold 25.4 GW, most of it in 36 plants that serve mines and smelters.

Sources: BIG, Global Energy Monitor, and news reports, compiled by KOMITMEN and compared with the Energy Ministry's totals by province. Switch the view to see capacity by use.

In West Java, two thirds of the capacity sits in four districts

West Java has 198 operating plants with 13.8 GW. Bekasi alone has 4.1 GW, and with Karawang, Purwakarta, and Cirebon it holds 68 percent of the province's capacity. Seven of the 27 districts have no plant at all.

Renewables provide 28 percent of West Java's capacity, well above the national 11 percent, mostly from the hydro dams on the Citarum river and from geothermal fields in the highlands.

Next steps and limits

The next step is to test which branch binds in which district, starting with West Java, and to link power supply and outages to growth and poverty.

The data have limits. Outages are reported by PLN unit, not by district, so they cannot separate districts within a province. The plant map counts installed capacity, not the power actually generated or delivered, and small or off-grid plants may be missing.
