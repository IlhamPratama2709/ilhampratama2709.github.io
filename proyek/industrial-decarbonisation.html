Summary

Indonesia's net zero target for 2050 depends on a few heavy industries: cement, steel, fertiliser, chemicals, and paper among them. This stream, carried out with an international research partner, studies which policies could cut their energy use and emissions without stalling growth.

The evidence starts from the BPS survey of large and medium manufacturers, which records energy use plant by plant. Before drawing any chart, the team checked those records year by year, and 6 of the 16 years show breaks. In 2010, electricity use at the same plants fell 39 percent, and in 2014 it rose 86 percent, while their workforce did not change.

Those years are breaks in reporting, not in production. So the dashboard compares sectors and regions within a year and does not show energy use as a trend.

The stream follows nine energy-intensive subsectors

The nine subsectors are those in the government's roadmap for industrial decarbonisation.

MaterialsCement; iron and steel; glass and ceramics; pulp and paper
ChemicalsFertiliser; other chemicals
Consumer goodsFood and beverages; textiles; automotive

Each subsector is defined by its KBLI industry codes. The dashboard uses whole code divisions, which is broader than the roadmap's product list, and shows both definitions side by side.

Electricity at the same plants fell 39 percent in 2010 and rose 86 percent in 2014, while employment stayed flat

Each year, the check matches every plant to its own record from the year before, between 14,700 and 22,700 plants a year. Electricity bought from PLN by those same plants fell 39 percent in 2010 and rose 86 percent in 2014. Their median employment did not change in any year.

Real factories do not lose a third of their power use without losing workers. Some of the jumps line up with known changes in the survey, such as a 42 percent rise in the number of plants covered in 2006.

So the six red years can be used only to compare sectors within that year. A trend is read only across the green years.

Source: BPS survey of large and medium manufacturing (IBS), 2000–2015, all plants; checks by KOMITMEN. Electricity is kWh bought from PLN. Hover over or tap a bar for the number of plants matched.

West Java has 43 of Indonesia's 143 industrial estates

The dashboard also maps where industry clusters. West Java has 43 industrial estates with 21,400 hectares, 31 of them in Bekasi, Karawang, and Purwakarta along the toll road east of Jakarta. Twenty-eight of them had started by 2015, the last year of the plant data.

Its only special economic zone, Lido in Bogor, was set up in 2021 for tourism, not manufacturing. Industry in West Java grew in estates, not in zones.

Next steps and limits

The next step is to agree on how to clean the plant records, and then to estimate energy intensity by subsector and region for the policy package.

The limits are serious. The survey covers plants with 20 or more workers. Energy is partly recorded as spending, so prices and quantities can be confused. The data end in 2015, so they describe the starting point of the transition, not its progress.

My role
